Synthetic Loan Portfolio Performance & Credit Risk Analytics (SD-186)

Context & Industry Problem:

Commercial banks, FinTechs, and risk analytics teams need granular, multi-table loan performance and payment waterfall data to stress-test PFRS 9 expected credit losses, calibrate default scoring models, and audit operational clearing lags. However, real financial records are strictly protected under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) and banking secrecy laws. SD-186 resolves this barrier by providing an empirically grounded, privacy-safe synthetic universe with realistic financial frictions.

Reflection

What I Learned:

Designing synthetic datasets for machine learning requires strictly decoupling underlying causal data-generating mechanisms (e.g. latent liquidity buffers U and informal debt W) from exported catalog columns. Scrubbing latent confounders pre-export induces authentic Omitted Variable Bias (OVB), preventing models from exploiting trivial formula inversions.

Challenges I Encountered:

Maintaining strict referential integrity and temporal consistency across 176,178 monthly servicing snapshots—specifically ensuring payments respect 5-stage priority waterfalls, interest rate resets adjust dynamically, and quarterly credit score updates avoid forward-looking data leakage.

What I'm Most Proud Of:

Building a cohesive, zero-cost (PHP 0.00 AI text spend) financial simulation engine in Google Colab that generates enterprise-scale Philippine banking datasets perfectly aligned with real-world macro stress dynamics and PFRS 9 provisioning while ensuring zero privacy risk.

What I Would Improve:

Expand the delinquency module to simulate formal loan restructuring programs (forbearance, tenor extensions) under BSP moratorium circulars, and introduce spatial geographic correlation across Philippine regions to model localized disaster-driven default shocks.
